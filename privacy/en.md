Last updated: 10 September 2026.

Aftervoice is an instrument for listening sessions. This policy says what happens to the data
the app produces. What it makes stays on the phone, except for a report you explicitly choose
to submit.

## In short

Aftervoice has no accounts, advertising or usage analytics. Recordings, the microphone track,
diary, notes, bookmarks and sensor data are never uploaded to our servers. Network access is
used for voice banks managed by Google Play and, only at your request, a text report.

## What stays on the phone

- **Your settings**: interface language, voice language, timer, auto-seek, noise filter.
- **The session diary**: date, listening time, the station used, the title and notes you write,
  and the bookmarks with the names you give them.
- **Recordings**, when you press Record. Room listening saves only the microphone, including
  nearby voices, with no audio output from the app. Tuning saves the app’s audio and, with
  permission, the microphone. The microphone may also pick up the speaker: the tracks are
  not acoustically isolated.

These are the app's private files, in its own storage. No other app on the phone can read them.
The app opts out of both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer: if you move to a new
phone, the diary does not follow you.

## What stays local

Recordings, microphone audio, the diary, settings, notes, bookmarks and sensor data stay on the
phone. There are no analytics, crash collection, advertising or tracking SDKs. The app contacts
the Altrove Labs server only when you press **Send report**.

The crash reports the author sees come from Google Play, aggregated and anonymous. They
describe how the app behaved, never what your sessions contained.

## Reports and support

If you tap **Report** on a recording, you write the reason inside Aftervoice. Only when you press
**Send report**, the app sends Altrove Labs the text, app version, interface language and random
session and recording identifiers over HTTPS. It sends no audio, microphone track, note,
bookmark, device identifier or email address.

The server necessarily sees the connection IP address. It uses it only in memory to limit abuse
to five reports per hour, never logs it, and forgets it within one hour or on restart. The
report is forwarded as an email to `support@altrovelabs.net` and retained only as long as needed
to assess it. The email service provider processes it only to deliver it to Altrove Labs. The
app shows a random reference you can use to request deletion by writing to support. The data is
not used for other purposes.

## The microphone permission

Aftervoice asks for one permission, **the microphone**, and uses it in two places:

- **During a recording**, to save the room alone or alongside the synthetic track in Tuning.
  The microphone opens when you press Record and closes when the recording ends, including
  when the app moves to the background.
- **In the sonar**, where what it hears is high-passed above 17 kHz inside the app before any
  processing, is not recorded, and is not kept. The microphone is open only while that screen
  is.

If you refuse permission, Room listening cannot record and explains how to allow access.
It never starts synthesis as a fallback. Tuning can record only the app’s audio and says so
explicitly. Sonar requires microphone access.

## How long data is kept, and how to remove it

Until you remove it. You can delete a single recording or a whole session from the archive,
with a confirmation. Uninstalling the app deletes everything: settings, diary and recordings.

## Children

Aftervoice is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data about them.

## The audio the app generates

The sounds Aftervoice makes are generated by a voice model running on the phone. They are not
recordings of real people and they are not evidence of anything.

Audio produced by the app carries a machine-readable mark declaring it synthetic, as Article
50 of the European AI Act requires. The mark is in the sound itself, not in a file tag: it
survives sharing, re-compression and a trim, and it is still there in a file that has been
renamed or re-exported. Anyone can check it, without asking us for anything, with the detector
described in [Checking the mark](../detector.html).

The mark carries nothing about you. There is no device identifier in it, no session
identifier, no installation identifier. It says one thing, always the same thing: a machine
made this audio. The microphone track of a recording is not marked, because it is not
generated audio — it is the room you were in.
